The problem

Langtang, 26 August 2026.
08:40 in the morning.

  1. AvalancheRock and ice break off the north face of Langtang Lirung, about 5,200 m up.
  2. DamThe debris blocks the Lhende Khola and a lake builds up behind it.
  3. BurstThe dam gives way. The Trishuli rises up to 9 m in 30 minutes, through Rasuwa and Nuwakot.
  4. SilenceCommunication towers go down before 09:00. The valleys go dark.
668 tourists from 34 countries out of contact the next day, most of them trekking
5,285 people still missing in Nepal six weeks later
15 days before helicopters could reach Tiru village

Why drones alone don't fix it

Drones can reach a valley in minutes, but here they share the valley's problem: the mountain blocks the radio. A drone that drops behind a ridge goes silent. If the hub drone coordinating the others fails, the whole search waits for orders that never come, and what the drones found never reaches the rescue team.

Sources: Wikipedia, 2026 Nepal–Tibet floods (missing as of 5 Oct; Tiru village) · Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026 (river rise; tourists out of contact).

The solution

No drone waits for orders.
No find is held by one drone alone.

Shared swarm memory

Every drone keeps its own copy of what the swarm knows: who has been found, which ground is searched, who is still flying. It swaps copies with every drone it meets, so a find that reaches one other drone survives losing the drone that made it.

No leader to lose

If the hub goes quiet for about 5 seconds, the drone with the lowest ID that can still hear others takes over. If the old hub comes back, it steps down. Search and reports never depended on it, so losing it costs nothing.

A swarm that heals itself

A neural cellular automaton, inspired by Google's self-repairing gecko, is trained on the formation. The drones compute it between them, each on its own patch. When one falls, its neighbours regrow its part.

Last resort

The data mule. If a drone has a report and no radio route to base, it flies the report home itself. The others wait their turn, so the report still gets home if the first carrier is lost.
